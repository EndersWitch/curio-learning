INTRO: We can write what someone said in two ways. <strong>Direct speech</strong> uses the exact words in quotation marks, and <strong>reported speech</strong> tells what was said without quoting it.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: Direct speech
CONTENT: Direct speech uses the exact words of the speaker, inside quotation marks. A comma follows the introductory verb and the first spoken word begins with a capital letter.
EXAMPLE: Mary said, "It is cold today."
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: Reported speech
CONTENT: Reported speech tells what was said without the quotation marks. We often add that, and the verb goes one stage back in tense.
EXAMPLE: Mary said that it was cold that day.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: What changes
CONTENT: In reported speech the pronouns change to keep the meaning, and time words change as well. Today becomes that day.
EXAMPLE: Joanne said, "I am feeling dizzy today."<br>Joanne said that she was feeling dizzy that day.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Report it
CONTENT: Change this to reported speech. Sipho said, "I am tired."
EXAMPLE: Sipho said that he was tired.
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:

INTRO: <strong>Direct speech</strong> uses the exact words of the speaker. <strong>Reported speech</strong> tells what was said without quoting it. This Grade 7 revision checks punctuation, pronouns, tenses and time words.

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Direct speech
CONTENT: Direct speech uses the exact words of the speaker, inside quotation marks. The introductory verb, such as said or asked, is followed by a comma. The first spoken word begins with a capital letter, and each new speaker starts a new paragraph.
EXAMPLE: Mary said, "It is cold today."<br>"How long until half-time?" asked an excited spectator.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: step_by_step
TITLE: Changing to reported speech
CONTENT: 1. Remove the quotation marks, question marks, exclamation marks and capital letters.<br>2. Add that, if or whether, or keep the question word.<br>3. Change the pronouns so the meaning stays the same.<br>4. Put the verb one stage back in tense when the introductory verb is in the past.<br>5. Change the time and place words.
EXAMPLE: Joanne said, "I am feeling dizzy today."<br>Joanne said that she was feeling dizzy that day.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: spot_difference
TITLE: Time and place words
SIDE_A: Direct
SIDE_B: Reported
CONTENT: here | there<br>now | then<br>today | that day<br>yesterday | the day before or the previous day<br>tomorrow | the next day or the following day<br>two days ago | two days before
EXAMPLE: These words change when the introductory verb is in the past tense.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Questions, commands and exclamations
CONTENT: Questions that begin with a question word keep that word. Yes or no questions take if or whether. Commands become to plus the verb. Exclamations such as ouch and hooray are described rather than repeated.
EXAMPLE: Roxanne asked why she couldn't have another candy floss.<br>The teacher ordered the children to do their homework.<br>The girl screamed in pain that she had stubbed her toe.
@@ENDCARD
