BASELINE ASSESSMENT: READING FLUENCY 
Norms Grade 4-7 Reading Fluency 
WORDS ACCURATELY READ PER MINUTE 
 
1. Learners to be given a text. 
2. Learners to read the text aloud for one minute (60 seconds).  
3. Address any errors made. [See below]. 
4. Educator to note how many errors were made and subtract these from the 
number of words accurately read. 
5. Hereafter, the mark allocation can be ascribed using the Norms for Reading 
Fluency. [Included below]. 
 
ERRORS may include the following: misinterpretation, substitutions, reversals, 
omissions and mispronunciation.  
❖ N.B. If there is no response from the learner after a waiting period of 
2-3 seconds, the learner must be prompted to continue. Mark the 
text where the learner stopped reading, after the full duration of one 
minute. The WORDS READ ACCURATELY PER MINUTE score can now 
be compared with the Fluency Norms.

SECTION B : 
 
MARKING GUIDELINES FOR READING COMPREHENSION/VISUAL TEXT 
o Spelling errors in one-mark-answers will only be penalised if it changes the 
meaning of the answer. 
o Spelling and language errors in longer answers cannot be penalised as the 
focus is on comprehension. 
o For open-ended questions, one cannot award marks for YES or NO or I AGREE, 
or I DISAGREE. The reason or motivation must be considered. 
o For TRUE or FALSE or FACT or OPINION questions, one mark is awarded for the 
reason, motivation, or quote. 
- 
If the learner has answered TRUE or FALSE correctly, but the motivation is 
incorrect, they are awarded only ONE mark. 
- 
If the learner has answered the TRUE or FALSE incorrectly, but the 
motivation is correct, NO MARKS are awarded. 
o No penalising of presence or lack of quotation marks, even if a quote was 
asked for. 
o Where a one-word-answer is requested and the candidate provides the 
answer in a full sentence, it is accepted, provided that the correct answer is 
underlined or highlighted in some way. 
o When two or three facts or marks are required and the candidate provides a 
whole list, only the first two or three facts will be marked. 
o Should the candidate use another language, other than the one used in the 
question paper, the foreign words must be ignored.   
- 
Should the answer, without the foreign words, makes sense, the candidate 
will not be penalised. 
- 
The answer will also be accepted if it requires a foreign word in the answer 
o Accept dialectal variations 
o For multiple choice questions, BOTH the letter AND the correct answer, written 
in full, must be accepted

MARKING GUIDELINES FOR LANGUAGE IN CONTEXT 
 
o One-word-answers are correct despite incorrect spelling unless the error 
changes the meaning of the word.  
o Incorrect spelling in sentences must be penalised if the error occurs within the 
sentence construction that is being assessed. 
o The use of correct punctuation with abbreviations is essential. 
o Sentence structures must be grammatically correct and in full sentences, as 
per instructions. 
o Both the letter and the corresponding answer are acceptable with multiple 
choice questions. 
 
❖ WORD STRUCTURES 
 
o Spelling must be correct in order for the mark to be awarded. 
o The standard variant applies 
o If only one word is required as an answer, the learner does not receive any 
marks if the instruction is not followed. However, if the learner writes a 
sentence, but identifies the correct word by underlining or highlighting it, 
award the mark. 
 
❖ SENTENCE STRUCTURES 
 
o Spelling does not count. 
o Regional language or dialect cannot be taken into consideration. 
o Should the instruction set out that the learner must begin the sentence in a 
specific manner, the WHOLE sentence must be written out in full. 
o PLEASE NOTE: With regard to advertisements and cartoons, language and 
spelling errors do not count, unless they impact on the meaning of the 
sentence.

The Dinosaur's Toothbrush by Anna Emm  
A long, long time ago, a huge dinosaur lived in an open grassy field. 
Every day he nibbled on the sweet, green grass, until his stomach was 
full. There he could stretch his long legs without knocking anything over, 
and he didn't bother anyone if he wanted to practise his loud dinosaur 
roar. Yep, the open grassland was definitely a very good home for a 
dinosaur like him!  
Not only was the dinosaur big and strong, but he also had a beautiful 
set of sharp, white teeth of which he was very proud! Every morning and 
every night he brushed his teeth with his giant toothbrush – back and 
forth, up and down, until the foam looked like fluffy clouds around his 
mouth. He made sure that his teeth were always shiny and healthy.  
One morning the dinosaur couldn't find his toothbrush! It wasn't on the 
big stone where he usually put it! He hurriedly began to look 
everywhere, for there were stalks of grass stuck between his teeth which 
bothered him terribly. He searched the entire grassy field, but he 
couldn't find his toothbrush. Grumbling, he then walked over to the 
rocky riverbank to look for his toothbrush there. All the little animals that 
lived along the river were shaking with fear. Frogs, ducklings and herons 
scurried in all directions as they tried to get away from his big feet. They 
could just tell that the dinosaur was in a bad mood…
