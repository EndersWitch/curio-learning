INTRO: <strong>Concord</strong> means agreement. The words in a sentence must match one another, and the most important match is between the <strong>subject</strong> and the <strong>verb</strong>. A singular subject needs a singular verb and a plural subject needs a plural verb.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: What is concord?
CONTENT: Concord, also called agreement, means the words in a sentence must match one another. The subject and the verb must agree in number and in person.
EXAMPLE: Joe eats his lunch.<br>Joe and Bobby eat their lunch.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: step_by_step
TITLE: Choosing the verb
CONTENT: 1. Find the subject.<br>2. Decide whether it is singular or plural.<br>3. Match the verb to the subject.
EXAMPLE: My brother and sister are coming for a visit.<br>The subject is my brother and sister, which is plural, so the verb is are.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Person and number
CONTENT: In the simple present the verb changes only for the third person singular, where it takes an s. Every other person uses the base form of the verb.
EXAMPLE: I eat, you eat, he eats<br>we eat, you eat, they eat
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Collective nouns and one subject
CONTENT: A collective noun refers to a group as a single unit, so it takes a singular verb. If one subject governs more than one verb, every verb must agree with it.
EXAMPLE: The class of students was kept in after school.<br>The film deals with the dangers of drug abuse and is aimed at teenagers.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Words between the subject and the verb
CONTENT: Do not match the verb to the nearest noun. Find the real subject first. Other words may come between the subject and the verb.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: The cost of the canoes are high.<br>Right: The cost of the canoes is high.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Pick the verb
CONTENT: Choose the correct verb. Thandi and her friends ___ at the park.
EXAMPLE: are. The subject is Thandi and her friends, which is plural.
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:
Say what concord means
Match the verb to a singular or plural subject
Use the correct form for each person
Treat a collective noun as a single unit
Find the subject when other words come between
@@ENDLEVEL

INTRO: Concord is not only about the verb. A <strong>pronoun</strong> must also agree with the noun it replaces, in number and gender. Some subjects, such as each and neither, look plural but need a singular verb.

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Pronouns agree with their nouns
CONTENT: A pronoun must agree with the noun it replaces in number and in gender. A singular noun takes a singular pronoun and a plural noun takes a plural pronoun.
EXAMPLE: The woman paid for her ticket.<br>The women paid for their tickets.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Words that take a singular verb
CONTENT: Some pronouns are always paired with a singular verb. These are anyone, everyone, nobody, no-one, somebody, someone, each, every, either and neither.
EXAMPLE: Everyone is here.<br>Neither of us is afraid of this responsibility.<br>Each of the learners has a book.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Group nouns
CONTENT: A group of things is one unit. The verb agrees with the word group, not with the plural noun that follows it.
EXAMPLE: A group of teenagers is planning a trip.<br>A bunch of flowers is on the table.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: The plural trap
CONTENT: A plural noun near the verb does not make the subject plural. Find the head word first.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: Each of the learners have a book.<br>Right: Each of the learners has a book.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Agree it
CONTENT: Choose the correct verb. Neither of the boys ___ late.
EXAMPLE: is. Neither takes a singular verb.
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:
Match a pronoun to its noun in number and gender
Use a singular verb after each, every, either and neither
Use a singular verb after group nouns
Find the head word of the subject
Spot agreement errors
@@ENDLEVEL
