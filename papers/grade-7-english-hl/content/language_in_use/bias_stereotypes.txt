INTRO: <strong>Bias</strong> or <strong>prejudice</strong> is a belief formed without evidence. It means judging a person or a group before you know the facts. Learning to spot it helps you read fairly and write fairly.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: Bias and prejudice
CONTENT: Bias or prejudice is a personal, pre-conceived belief not based on evidence or fact. It is literally pre-judging, which means making up your mind about something or someone before you know the relevant facts.
EXAMPLE: Women are bad drivers.<br>This claim judges a whole group without evidence.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Where bias comes from
CONTENT: Bias often results from ignorance or from generalisations. A generalisation treats every member of a group as if they were the same.
EXAMPLE: All teenagers are lazy.<br>Boys never listen.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Words that give bias away
CONTENT: Words such as all, never, always, everyone and nobody often signal a generalisation. Emotive words that praise one side and insult the other also signal bias.
EXAMPLE: Our brilliant team crushed the useless visitors.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Opinion based on evidence
CONTENT: A view that is backed by evidence is not prejudice. Prejudice is a belief formed without looking at the facts.
EXAMPLE: Based on the survey, most learners prefer maths. (evidence)<br>Nobody likes maths. (prejudice)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Spot the bias
CONTENT: Explain why this statement is biased. All boys are noisy.
EXAMPLE: It judges every boy as the same without evidence.
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:
Say what bias and prejudice mean
Recognise generalisations
Spot words that signal bias
Tell evidence from prejudice
Recognise biased headlines
@@ENDLEVEL

INTRO: A <strong>stereotype</strong> is a fixed idea about a whole group of people. Fair writers avoid stereotypes and choose language that respects people's age, gender, race and abilities.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: What is a stereotype?
CONTENT: A stereotype is a fixed, oversimplified idea about a whole group of people. It treats every person in the group as the same. The Handbook does not define the word, so this card uses the usual meaning.
EXAMPLE: All old people are forgetful.<br>Boys never cry.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Why stereotypes are harmful
CONTENT: Stereotypes ignore the differences between people. They can lead to unfair treatment and they are a form of bias.
EXAMPLE: Some old people enjoy gardening and some do not.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Fair language
CONTENT: In modern times we avoid words that insult people because of age, gender, race, social status, physical appearance or physical or intellectual disabilities. We often replace offensive words with gentler words, called euphemisms.
EXAMPLE: chairman or chairwoman, chairperson<br>old people, senior citizens
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Titles
CONTENT: Many women prefer the title Ms because it does not show whether they are married.
EXAMPLE: My teacher is Ms Grey.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Make it fair
CONTENT: Rewrite this sentence so that it does not stereotype anyone. All girls are afraid of mice.
EXAMPLE: Some people are afraid of mice.
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:
Say what a stereotype is
Recognise stereotypes in sentences
Say why stereotypes are harmful
Choose fair and respectful language
Use euphemisms and neutral titles
@@ENDLEVEL
