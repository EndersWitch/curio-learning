INTRO: Sometimes one word can say what a whole phrase says. A child whose parents have died is an <strong>orphan</strong>. Using the exact word makes your writing shorter and clearer.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: One word for a phrase
CONTENT: Many phrases have a single word with the same meaning. Using that word makes a sentence shorter. The Handbook does not cover this topic, so this level uses well-known words.
EXAMPLE: a person who walks, a pedestrian<br>a child whose parents are dead, an orphan
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Animals and what they eat
CONTENT: Special words name animals by what they eat.
EXAMPLE: herbivore, an animal that eats only plants<br>carnivore, an animal that eats meat<br>omnivore, an animal that eats both plants and meat
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: People and activities
CONTENT: Many one-word replacements name a kind of person or a state.
EXAMPLE: orphan, a child whose parents are both dead<br>pedestrian, a person who walks<br>vegetarian, a person who does not eat meat<br>novice, a person who is new to a job or activity<br>nocturnal, active at night
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Prefixes help
CONTENT: A prefix in the word can give you the meaning. The prefix bi means two, auto means self and mono means one.
EXAMPLE: bilingual, able to speak two languages<br>autobiography, the story of your own life
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Say it in one word
CONTENT: Write one word for an animal that eats only plants.
EXAMPLE: herbivore.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: People and writing
CONTENT: Many phrases describing people or writing have one-word replacements. Prefixes such as auto, mono and bi help you work out the meaning.
EXAMPLE: biography, the story of a person's life written by someone else<br>autobiography, the story of your own life<br>monologue, a long speech by one person<br>anonymous, of unknown name<br>unanimous, agreed by everyone
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Time and animals
CONTENT: Some words name a length of time, and others name animals by their habits.
EXAMPLE: decade, ten years<br>century, one hundred years<br>millennium, one thousand years<br>nocturnal, active at night<br>carnivore, herbivore and omnivore
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: step_by_step
TITLE: Finding the exact word
CONTENT: 1. Read the phrase and decide what it describes.<br>2. Look for a prefix or root you know.<br>3. Test each choice by putting it back into the sentence.<br>4. Pick the word that fits the whole phrase.
EXAMPLE: a person who is new to a job<br>The word is novice, since the others describe experts or walkers.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Words that look alike
CONTENT: Some words in this group are easy to mix up. Biography and autobiography differ by who wrote the story, and carnivore, herbivore and omnivore differ by what the animal eats.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: A herbivore eats meat.<br>Right: A carnivore eats meat.
@@ENDCARD
