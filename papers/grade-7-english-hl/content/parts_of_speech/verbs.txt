INTRO: A <strong>verb</strong> is a doing or action word, or a word that shows a state of being. A <strong>finite verb</strong> has a subject, a number and a tense, and it can anchor a sentence. The <strong>infinitive</strong> is the non-finite form that begins with to.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: What is a verb?
CONTENT: A verb is an action word or a word that shows a state of being. A good test is to ask can you followed by the word. You can run, ask and reply, so these are verbs. You can also put a pronoun in front of a verb.
EXAMPLE: The boy runs. She is happy.<br>He runs. She asked. They replied.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Finite verbs
CONTENT: A finite verb can stand on its own and does not need a helping verb. It must have a subject, show number (singular or plural) and show tense. Together with the subject, it makes a complete sentence.
EXAMPLE: She plays. They argue.<br>The girl plays netball. The girls play netball.<br>Today I play. Yesterday I played.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: The infinitive
CONTENT: When a verb is preceded by to, it is called the infinitive. The infinitive cannot stand alone in a sentence. It needs a finite verb in front of it.
EXAMPLE: to play, to argue, to study<br>She wants to leave.<br>They start to argue.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Fragments with no finite verb
CONTENT: A group of words with only an infinitive or an -ing form is not a complete sentence. The Handbook says that an -ing word needs a helping verb in front of it.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: The tap leaking. To leave.<br>Right: The tap is leaking. She wants to leave.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Find the finite verb
CONTENT: Write the finite verb in this sentence. Children love to play.
EXAMPLE: love. It has a subject, children, and shows the simple present tense. To play is the infinitive and cannot stand alone.
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:

@@ENDLEVEL

INTRO: An <strong>auxiliary verb</strong> is a helping verb. It comes before another verb and helps to show tense. The same word can be a helping verb in one sentence and the main verb in another, so you have to look at how it is used.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: Auxiliary verbs
CONTENT: An auxiliary verb is a helping verb. It always comes before a main verb and helps to regulate the tense of the sentence. The Handbook lists the common ones as am, are, be, can, could, had, has, have, is, may, might, must, shall, should, was, were, will and would.
EXAMPLE: She has done her work.<br>We could see the sun rising in the east.<br>They will leave tomorrow.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Finite or auxiliary
CONTENT: The same verb can be finite or auxiliary depending on the sentence. If it stands alone as the verb, it is a finite verb. If it helps another verb, it is an auxiliary verb.
EXAMPLE: He is the president of his company. (is is the finite verb)<br>He is hoping to become president. (is helps hoping)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: The linking verb
CONTENT: The verb to be (is, am, are) is also called the linking verb. It connects a noun with another noun, or a noun with an adjective. The word after it is not an object. It is called the complement.
EXAMPLE: The man is a soldier. (noun)<br>The man is brave. (adjective)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Participles need help
CONTENT: A word such as leaking or played cannot stand alone as the verb in a sentence. It needs an auxiliary verb in front of it. Without the auxiliary the sentence is incomplete.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: The tap leaking.<br>Right: The tap is leaking.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Spot the helper
CONTENT: Write the auxiliary verb. Lerato has finished her homework.
EXAMPLE: has. It helps the main verb finished and shows the tense.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD

TITLE: Kinds of verb
SIDE_A: Kind
SIDE_B: Example
CONTENT: Finite verb | She plays netball.<br>Infinitive | to play<br>Auxiliary verb | She has played.<br>Linking verb | She is happy.
EXAMPLE: A word like is can be finite, auxiliary or linking. Look at what it does in the sentence.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: step_by_step
TITLE: Analysing a verb group
CONTENT: 1. Find the main verb, the word that names the action or state.<br>2. Look in front of it for helping verbs.<br>3. Check whether a to comes before the verb, which makes it an infinitive.<br>4. Find the verb that has a subject, number and tense, which is the finite verb.
EXAMPLE: She has decided to leave.<br>Has is the auxiliary, decided is the main verb, and to leave is the infinitive.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: What a sentence needs
CONTENT: A complete sentence needs a finite verb. An infinitive or a participle on its own is not enough. A participle needs an auxiliary verb in front of it.
EXAMPLE: She wants to leave. (finite verb wants)<br>The tap is leaking. (auxiliary is)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Common verb mistakes
CONTENT: The usual errors are treating the infinitive as the finite verb and leaving out the auxiliary before an -ing word. Another is thinking every is, was or have is an auxiliary verb.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: The tap leaking.<br>Right: The tap is leaking.
@@ENDCARD
