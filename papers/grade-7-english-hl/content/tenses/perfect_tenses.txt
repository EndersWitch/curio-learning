INTRO: The <strong>present perfect tense</strong> links an earlier action to the present. It is built from <em>have</em> or <em>has</em> plus the <strong>past participle</strong> of the verb. Knowing which verbs have irregular participles is the key skill.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: What is the present perfect?
CONTENT: The present perfect tense shows an action that is already completed, usually without saying exactly when. It is formed with have or has and the past participle. The Handbook describes perfect tenses as showing what is already completed.
EXAMPLE: I have driven.<br>Thabo has finished his homework.<br>We have visited Kimberley.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Have or has
CONTENT: Use has with he, she, it and singular nouns. Use have with I, you, we, they and plural nouns. The past participle does not change for different subjects. Negatives use have not or has not, and the short forms are haven't and hasn't.
EXAMPLE: She has eaten.<br>They have eaten.<br>He hasn't eaten.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: spot_difference
TITLE: Regular and irregular participles
SIDE_A: Regular (same as simple past)
SIDE_B: Irregular (own form)
CONTENT: walk → walked | eat → eaten<br>play → played | write → written<br>finish → finished | go → gone<br>clean → cleaned | break → broken
EXAMPLE: Regular verbs use the same form for the simple past and the participle. Irregular verbs have to be learned. The Handbook gives drive → driven in I have driven.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Signal words
CONTENT: Words such as already, just, yet, ever, never, since and for often go with the present perfect. Do not use it with a finished time such as yesterday or last week. Those need the simple past.
EXAMPLE: I have already seen it.<br>She has lived here since 2020.<br>Wrong: We have visited Soweto yesterday.<br>Right: We visited Soweto yesterday.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Simple past in place of the participle
CONTENT: Many learners use the simple past form after have or has. For irregular verbs the two forms are different, so this gives a wrong sentence.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: She has went home. He has ate lunch.<br>Right: She has gone home. He has eaten lunch.
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:
Form the present perfect with have and has plus a participle
Choose has or have to match the subject
Write common irregular past participles
Use signal words such as already, just and since
Know that the present perfect does not go with yesterday or last week
@@ENDLEVEL

INTRO: The <strong>past perfect tense</strong> shows an action that was completed before another action or time that is already finished. It uses <em>had</em> plus the past participle. It helps the reader see which of two events came first.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: What is the past perfect?
CONTENT: The past perfect tense describes an action that was already completed before another event. It is formed with had and the past participle. Had is used for every subject. The Handbook example is I had driven.
EXAMPLE: I had driven.<br>Sipho had finished his homework before supper.<br>The train had left when we reached the station.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Two actions, two tenses
CONTENT: When two earlier actions are described, the one that happened first goes in the past perfect. The one that happened second goes in the simple past. Words such as before, after, by the time and already often show the order.
EXAMPLE: When Amara arrived, the match had started. (started first)<br>After Thabo had eaten, he went outside. (ate first)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: example
TITLE: Reading the order
CONTENT: In each pair, the past perfect verb is the earlier event. The simple past verb is the later one. Reversing the tenses reverses the meaning.
EXAMPLE: The bus left before Lerato arrived. (the bus left first)<br>The bus had left before Lerato arrived. (the bus was already gone)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Had plus the wrong form
CONTENT: After had, use the past participle. Many irregular verbs have different forms for the simple past and the participle, so the simple past form is wrong after had.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: She had went home. The bell had rang.<br>Right: She had gone home. The bell had rung.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Which came first
CONTENT: Read this sentence and name the first event. By the time Nomsa reached school, the assembly had ended.
EXAMPLE: The assembly ended first. Had ended is the past perfect, so it happened before Nomsa reached school.
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:
Form the past perfect with had and a participle
Identify which of two earlier actions came first
Use signal words such as before, after and by the time
Use correct irregular participles after had
Tell the past perfect from the simple past
@@ENDLEVEL

INTRO: The <strong>future perfect tense</strong> looks ahead to a time and says an action will be completed by then. It is built from <em>will have</em> plus the past participle. Time phrases such as <em>by Friday</em> are the clue.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: What is the future perfect?
CONTENT: The future perfect tense shows an action that will already be completed by a stated future time. It uses will have (or shall have with I and we) plus the past participle. The Handbook example is I shall have driven.
EXAMPLE: I shall have driven to the coast by this time tomorrow.<br>By Friday, Naledi will have written her essay.<br>They will have left by noon.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Will have plus a participle
CONTENT: Both helping words are needed, will and have. The verb that follows must be the past participle. The form does not change for different subjects. Negatives put not after will, and questions put will first.
EXAMPLE: She will have finished.<br>They will not have arrived.<br>Will you have finished?
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: spot_difference
TITLE: Three future tenses compared
SIDE_A: Tense
SIDE_B: Meaning
CONTENT: Simple future, I will drive | The action will happen<br>Future continuous, I will be driving | The action will be in progress<br>Future perfect, I will have driven | The action will be finished by then
EXAMPLE: The Handbook compares these three with the coast example, shall drive, shall be driving and shall have driven.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: The participle is needed
CONTENT: After will have, use the past participle and not the simple past or the base verb. For irregular verbs the participle is a different form.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: Mia will have gave the speech. Danie will have wrote it.<br>Right: Mia will have given the speech. Danie will have written it.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Finished by then
CONTENT: Complete the sentence. By next Friday, I (finish) my project.
EXAMPLE: By next Friday, I will have finished my project. By next Friday gives the future time and finish is regular, so the participle is finished.
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:
Form the future perfect with will have and a participle
Use time phrases such as by Friday and by the end of the year
Make negatives and questions in the future perfect
Tell the future perfect from the simple future and the future continuous
Use correct irregular participles after will have
@@ENDLEVEL
