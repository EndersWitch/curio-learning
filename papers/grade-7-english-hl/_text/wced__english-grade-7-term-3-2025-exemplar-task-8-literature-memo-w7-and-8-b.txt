5

Die monster. deur Elizabeth Bekker  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Summary: A conversation between six friends about their weekend's events. 
 
Age group: 12-13 year olds 
 
Characters: Pradesh, Joe, Bridget, Anna, Busi and Sam 
 
Set: Six friends sit in a semi-circle on one side of the stage. They are dressed comfortably. 
Certain friends' faces look like they're angry at the world. It is pretty sunny and the sounds of the 
birds come through clearly. Suddenly, the company between the six friends takes a sharp turn 
when the topic starts to dwell on money. 
  
Stage directions: In italics and in brackets (...) in each speaking turn. 
 
°°°°°° 
1. PRADESH:   
(Dejected) What did you do this weekend, Joe? 
2. JOE:  
 
(Shows little interest) Not much. I was so bored! There is nothing to do in 
our house. And we couldn't go anywhere. And you? 
3. PRADESH:   
(Looks down.) I wanted to go to the beach to surf, but the weather was too 
bad. 
4. BRIDGET:  
 
(Excited.) I love the rain. (Shakes her hair.) I went to the movies (head in 
the light) and spent the rest of the weekend with my nose in a book. 
(Smiles broadly.) 
5. ANNA:   
 
Oh! I also love to read… 
6. PRADESH:  
(Annoyed) ...no one asked you guys!!!

I am sorry. by E.K.M. Dido 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
1. The two teenage boys sit hunched over with their hands limp between their outstretched 
legs on the cool, white marble stone. Every now and then one gives a heavy sigh. Another 
picks up a hand full of sharp, gray pebbles next to him, and lets them slip out of his hand one 
by one. 
2. It's quiet and warm where they sit, but both of their white school shirts are buttoned up to 
the neck. Here and there the sun burns down on them through the leaves of an oak tree. A 
sunspot falls on one of the boys' dusty black school shoes; he slowly pulls his leg up and 
hugs his knee and sighs. In the tree branches far above them a pigeon coos. A sparrow floats 
out of the blue sky to the ground and sits and chirps on a marble slab not far from them. The 
boys don't pay attention to the sparrow. Nor do they pay attention to the distant rumble of car 
engines and trucks. They just sit , hunched over, on the marble stone. 
3. "Old Dingo's new kaia looks funny now that the ground is level and the marble is up, eh 
bra Mike?" one breaks the silence. 
4. "That's right, Charlie," replied Mike, turning his head sideways and looking at the 
headstone of the marble. His lips trembled slightly as he spoke again. "I'm really sorry, Dingo. 
You will never know how sorry! I never meant to drag you in. I..." He stopped talking and 
rubbed his hands together. 
 
5. "I'm so sorry, my bra," Charlie said softly and picked up pebbles again and watched them 
fall from his hand one by one.

6. For a long time they sat still again. Each with their own thoughts. Then, as if reading each 
other's thoughts, they swung their heads towards the writing on the headstone, and began to 
read softly but clearly what was chiseled out in large, thick, black writing on the white marble:  
JOHN MATTHEW DINGO  
2 Dec. 1988 - 24 Aug. 2005 
"For a short time you were on loan to us, but forever we will remember you." 
7. “I'm really sorry, Dingo. I miss you terribly!” Mike said, then looked at Charlie. "Do you think 
old Dingo can hear us?" he asked.  
Charlie doesn't answer right away. His eyes are shining and he first blows his nose on his 
left shirt sleeve. "I don't know, my bra," he answered with a shaky voice. "But I think so." 
8. Mike's head jerked towards his friend. "Really?" he asked. His voice sounded hopeful. He 
took a quick look at the marble headstone and then looked at Charlie again. 
9. "Yeah, I think bra Dingo can hear," repeated Charlie. "I was on a vibe once. Long before 
the stuff happened. That day I quickly took a few hits of tik with the men at the corner shop. 
I don't know where you and bra Dingo were because you weren't around..." 
10. "Charlie, get to the point!" Mike interrupted him.  
"Okay. sorry. Anyway, you know how that stuff gets me talking. Babbling non-stop, you know! 
I tell you my brother, I wanted to be silent, but my tongue was wagging so badly that no one 
could silence me. Back at home I had to shove chewing gum in my mouth to keep quiet. 
Because the aunty I was living with was checking me out funny, I felt obliged to sit next to her 
on the sofa and watch her programme. It was quite a church program!” he said with a touch 
of annoyance in his voice. His friend's mouth opened, but he continued to speak quickly. 
"Yes-no, as I say, there I sat in a lekker vibe next to the aunty, watching TV about a bunch of 
ministers who were arguing about dead people... sorry, bra Dingo," he quickly apologises to 
the headstone before he continues to speak. 
11. But before he could say another word, Mike gave a loud sob and went to kneel by the 
headstone. His tears came quickly and streamed down his cheeks. "I'm sorry, Dingo. So 
sorry!”, he kept repeating through the sobs. When he calmed down, he rose from his knees 
and sat tightly against Charlie on the ridge of the marble headstone. 
Mike looked straight ahead, without blinking. After a long time he asks again, without 
looking at Charlie: "What did the ministers have to say?"  
“I miss him too, old Mike. We'll never find a pal like him again," comforts Charlie and puts 
an arm around his friend's shoulder.
