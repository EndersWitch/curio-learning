@@CARD
TYPE: spot_difference
TITLE: The four sentence types
SIDE_A: Type
SIDE_B: Job
CONTENT: Statement | supplies information<br>Question | asks for information<br>Command (imperative) | gives an order or instruction<br>Exclamation | expresses emotion such as surprise, fear, alarm or anger
EXAMPLE: Shaun is going to school.<br>Where is Shaun going?<br>Do not enter through that door.<br>It's a ghost!
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Look at the job, not the word
CONTENT: A question word does not always make a question. Decide what the sentence does. A polite request that asks for information is still a question, and a polite order is still a command.
EXAMPLE: I know what you mean. (statement)<br>What do you mean? (question)<br>Please close the window. (command)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: step_by_step
TITLE: Naming the type
CONTENT: 1. Ask whether the sentence gives information, asks for it, gives an order or shows strong feeling.<br>2. Check the punctuation at the end.<br>3. Check whether the word order has changed.
EXAMPLE: Leave the room immediately!<br>It gives an order, so it is a command, and the exclamation mark adds force.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Exclamation marks
CONTENT: An exclamation mark does not make every sentence an exclamation. A command can end with one to add force. An exclamation expresses emotion.
EXAMPLE: Leave the room immediately! (command)<br>It's a ghost! (exclamation)
@@ENDCARD

Which sentence is a statement?
A: Go to school.
B: Where is Shaun going?

A statement supplies information. The other sentences ask, order or express feeling.
