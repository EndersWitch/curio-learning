INTRO: A <strong>pun</strong> is a clever play on words, and <strong>ambiguity</strong> is when a sentence can be understood in more than one way. This Grade 7 revision checks that you can spot both.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: Puns
CONTENT: A pun is a clever play on words that are alike in sound but different in meaning. The double meaning is used to create humour. Puns are common in jokes, riddles and headlines.
EXAMPLE: Why did the tennis player have insomnia? There was a racquet in the room.<br>Cricket captain stumped!
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: Ambiguity
CONTENT: A sentence is ambiguous when it can be understood in two or more ways. It often happens when a pronoun could refer to more than one person.
EXAMPLE: John told James that his brother had arrived.<br>Whose brother had arrived, James's or John's?
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: step_by_step
TITLE: Removing ambiguity
CONTENT: 1. Find the word that could mean more than one thing.<br>2. Decide what you really mean.<br>3. Replace the unclear pronoun with a name or rewrite the sentence.
EXAMPLE: John told James that John's brother had arrived.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Pun or mistake
CONTENT: A pun is deliberate and aims to amuse. A wrong word used by mistake is not a pun. Check whether the double meaning is intended.
EXAMPLE: Cricket captain stumped! uses stumped for both a cricket dismissal and being puzzled.
@@ENDCARD
