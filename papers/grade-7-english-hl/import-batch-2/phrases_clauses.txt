INTRO: A <strong>phrase</strong> has no finite verb, and a <strong>clause</strong> has one. This Grade 7 revision checks that you can tell them apart, find main and subordinate clauses, and name the type of each.

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Types of phrase
CONTENT: Adjectival phrases tell us more about a noun. Noun phrases answer who or what. Adverbial phrases modify the verb and tell how, when, where or why.
EXAMPLE: The model with the dazzling smile came from Durban. (adjectival)<br>The girl entered the room at nine o'clock. (adverbial, time)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Types of subordinate clause
CONTENT: A noun clause does the job of a noun and can be replaced by it or that. An adjectival clause qualifies a noun and usually begins with who, which, that, whom or whose. An adverbial clause does the job of an adverb and tells time, place, manner, reason, concession, result or condition.
EXAMPLE: What they learnt at school helped them. (noun)<br>The girl who had the longest hair won. (adjectival)<br>While the engine was running, the man jumped out. (adverbial, time)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: A group of words is not always a clause
CONTENT: A group of words with no finite verb is a phrase, however long it is. A clause must have a finite verb of its own.
EXAMPLE: The shop around the corner is a phrase.<br>The shop is open is a clause.
@@ENDCARD

@@Q
DIFFICULTY: Starter
QUESTION:
A clause is a group of words that contains
A: no verb
B: a finite verb
C: only nouns
D: only adjectives
CORRECT: B
EXPLAIN:
A clause always contains a finite verb. A group of words without one is a phrase.
@@ENDQ

@@Q
DIFFICULTY: Starter
QUESTION:
Which group of words is a phrase?
A: The shop opened
B: The shop is open
C: The shop around the corner
D: The shop opens at nine
CORRECT: C
EXPLAIN:
The shop around the corner has no finite verb, so it is a phrase. The other groups each have a finite verb.
@@ENDQ

@@Q
DIFFICULTY: Starter
QUESTION:
A main clause is a clause that
A: only asks a question
B: cannot stand alone
C: has no verb
D: can stand alone and make complete sense
CORRECT: D
EXPLAIN:
A main clause makes complete sense on its own. A subordinate clause depends on it.
@@ENDQ

@@Q
DIFFICULTY: Starter
QUESTION:
A subordinate clause is a clause that
A: can stand alone
B: depends on the main clause for its meaning
C: has no finite verb
D: is always a question
CORRECT: B
EXPLAIN:
A subordinate clause contains a verb but cannot stand alone.
@@ENDQ

Which sentence contains a subordinate clause of concession?
A: The girl read her book.
B: The girl, although she was tired, read her book.
C: The girl read her book in the morning.
D: The girl read her book quickly.
CORRECT: B
EXPLAIN:
