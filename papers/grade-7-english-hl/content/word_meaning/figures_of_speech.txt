INTRO: <strong>Figures of speech</strong> create pictures in the mind. This Grade 7 revision covers comparisons, sound devices, exaggeration and understatement, and contradictions.

@@CARD
TYPE: spot_difference
TITLE: Comparisons
SIDE_A: Figure
SIDE_B: Example
CONTENT: Simile, a direct comparison with as or like | He is as wealthy as Bill Gates.<br>Metaphor, a comparison without as or like | She is a bubble in a bottle of champagne.<br>Personification, human qualities given to things | The clouds looked down and wept on the earth.
EXAMPLE: A metaphor says one thing is another. A simile says one thing is like another.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Sound devices
CONTENT: Alliteration repeats consonant sounds at the beginning of words. Assonance repeats vowel sounds. Onomatopoeia uses words that imitate real sounds. Rhyme depends on sound.
EXAMPLE: Football fever fuels fans. (alliteration)<br>crash, bang, hiss (onomatopoeia)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Exaggeration and understatement
CONTENT: Hyperbole is an over-exaggeration not meant to be taken literally. Litotes uses a negative and an opposite to understate. A euphemism softens an unpleasant idea.
EXAMPLE: She prepared enough food to feed an army. (hyperbole)<br>He is no Einstein. (litotes)<br>He passed away. (euphemism)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Contradictions
CONTENT: An oxymoron places two seemingly contradictory words next to each other. Irony implies the opposite of what is said. Sarcasm is irony used to hurt or insult.
EXAMPLE: a pretty ugly sight (oxymoron)<br>I can't wait for my detention on Friday afternoon. (irony)
@@ENDCARD

@@Q
DIFFICULTY: Starter
QUESTION:
Which sentence contains a simile?
A: He has money.
B: He is a Bill Gates.
C: He is wealthy.
D: He is as wealthy as Bill Gates.
CORRECT: D
EXPLAIN:
A simile compares directly using as or like. A metaphor makes the comparison without those words.
@@ENDQ

@@Q
DIFFICULTY: Starter
QUESTION:
Which sentence contains a metaphor?
A: The classroom was like a zoo.
B: The classroom was a zoo.
C: The classroom was as noisy as a zoo.
D: The classroom was noisy.
CORRECT: B
EXPLAIN:
A metaphor says one thing is another. The other sentences use like or as, or make no comparison.
@@ENDQ

Which sentence contains personification?

B: The clouds were grey.
C: The clouds looked down and wept on the earth.

EXPLAIN:
Looking down and weeping are human actions given to clouds, which is personification.
