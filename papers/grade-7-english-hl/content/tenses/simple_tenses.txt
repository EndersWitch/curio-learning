INTRO: The <strong>simple present tense</strong> tells us what happens regularly or what is always true. Sipho walks to school every day. The sun rises in the east. Getting the verb ending right is the main skill here.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: What is the simple present?
CONTENT: The simple present tense shows habits, routines and facts. It uses the base form of the verb. With he, she, it and singular nouns, the verb takes an -s or -es ending.
EXAMPLE: I paint a picture.<br>Sipho walks to school every day.<br>Snakes are reptiles.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Adding -s and -es
CONTENT: Most verbs just add -s (play, plays). Verbs ending in s, sh, ch, x, z or o add -es. A consonant followed by y changes to -ies. A vowel followed by y just adds -s. The verb have becomes has.
EXAMPLE: watch → watches<br>go → goes<br>study → studies<br>play → plays<br>have → has
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: example
TITLE: When we use it
CONTENT: Use the simple present for habits, for facts that are always true and for timetables. Signal words such as every day, always, usually, often, never and on Mondays often point to this tense.
EXAMPLE: Lerato always packs her bag at night. (habit)<br>Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius. (fact)<br>The train leaves at six. (timetable)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Doesn't takes no -s
CONTENT: After does not or doesn't, the verb goes back to its base form. The -s ending is already carried by does. Writing doesn't likes is wrong.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: Sipho doesn't likes boerewors.<br>Right: Sipho doesn't like boerewors.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Fix the verb
CONTENT: Choose the correct verb. Nomsa (go / goes) to Soweto Primary every day.
EXAMPLE: goes. Nomsa is one person (she), and go ends in o, so it adds -es.
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:
Identify a sentence in the simple present tense
Add -s or -es to a verb with he, she and it
Choose between the base form and the -s form after plural subjects
Know that habits, routines and facts use the simple present
Use the base verb after does not and doesn't
@@ENDLEVEL

INTRO: The <strong>simple past tense</strong> describes an action that started and finished at a definite time before now. Most verbs add -ed, but many common verbs change in their own way. Spelling and irregular forms are where marks are lost.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: What is the simple past?
CONTENT: The simple past tense shows an action that is finished. The time is often named or clear from the sentence. Regular verbs add -ed or -d. Irregular verbs have their own forms.
EXAMPLE: I painted a picture yesterday.<br>Thabo kicked the ball.<br>We ate pap last night.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Spelling regular verbs
CONTENT: Most verbs add -ed. Verbs ending in e add only -d. A consonant followed by y changes to -ied. A vowel followed by y just adds -ed. A short verb with one vowel and one final consonant doubles that consonant.
EXAMPLE: walk → walked<br>live → lived<br>carry → carried<br>play → played<br>stop → stopped
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: spot_difference
TITLE: Regular and irregular verbs
SIDE_A: Regular (add -ed)
SIDE_B: Irregular (new form)
CONTENT: walk → walked | go → went<br>jump → jumped | eat → ate<br>clean → cleaned | buy → bought<br>play → played | swim → swam
EXAMPLE: Irregular verbs have to be learned. The Handbook gives eat → ate and drive → drove as examples.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Did already shows the past
CONTENT: In questions and negatives, did carries the past tense. The main verb goes back to its base form. Did not went and did you saw are both wrong.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: Nomsa didn't ate lunch.<br>Right: Nomsa didn't eat lunch.<br>Right: Did you see the match?
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Spot the finished action
CONTENT: Write the simple past of these verbs. try, drop, see, play.
EXAMPLE: tried (y after a consonant becomes ied), dropped (the final consonant doubles), saw (irregular), played (y after a vowel just adds ed).
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:
Write the simple past of regular verbs
Apply the spelling rules for -ed endings
Recognise common irregular past forms
Use the base verb after did and didn't
Use signal words such as yesterday, last week and ago
@@ENDLEVEL

INTRO: The <strong>simple future tense</strong> talks about actions that have not happened yet. It is built with <em>will</em> or <em>shall</em> followed by the base verb. The verb after will never changes, whoever the subject is.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: What is the simple future?
CONTENT: The simple future tense shows an action that will happen later. It is formed with will or shall plus the base form of the verb. Will works with every subject. Shall is used mainly with I and we.
EXAMPLE: I shall paint a picture tomorrow.<br>Naledi will visit her gran on Sunday.<br>They will arrive in Polokwane at noon.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Will plus the base verb
CONTENT: The verb after will or shall never takes -s, -ed or -ing. Negatives use will not or the short form won't. Questions put will first. The short form of will is 'll, as in she'll and they'll.
EXAMPLE: She will walk. (not will walks)<br>We will not forget. We won't forget.<br>Will you come?<br>They'll arrive soon.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: example
TITLE: All three simple tenses
CONTENT: Compare one verb in the three simple tenses. The Handbook shows this pattern with paint, and it works for any verb.
EXAMPLE: Present, I paint a picture today.<br>Past, I painted a picture yesterday.<br>Future, I shall paint a picture tomorrow.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Only one tense marker
CONTENT: Will already shows the future. Adding -s or -ed to the verb after it is a mistake. Putting will after the verb is also wrong.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: Danie will goes to Pretoria.<br>Wrong: Danie will went to Pretoria.<br>Right: Danie will go to Pretoria.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Make it future
CONTENT: Rewrite in the simple future. Lerato writes her test on Monday.
EXAMPLE: Lerato will write her test on Monday. Will goes before the base verb write, and the -s disappears.
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:
Form the simple future with will and shall
Use the base verb after will
Make negatives with will not and won't
Use the short form 'll correctly
Recognise future signal words such as tomorrow and next week
@@ENDLEVEL

CONTENT: When a passage is about one time, the verbs should stay in one tense. A sentence that jumps to a different tense without a time signal is usually a mistake.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: Yesterday Tariq walks to the shop and buys bread.<br>Right: Yesterday Tariq walked to the shop and bought bread.
@@ENDCARD

Dineo must rewrite Every day Tebogo walked to school in the simple present.<br>Sipho wrote Every day Tebogo walk to school.<br>Amara wrote Every day Tebogo walks to school.<br>Naledi wrote Every day Tebogo will walk to school.<br>Who is correct?
A: Sipho
B: Amara
C: Naledi
D: None of them
CORRECT: B
EXPLAIN:
Amara used walks, the simple present form for a singular subject. Sipho left off the -s. Naledi changed the sentence to the simple future.
