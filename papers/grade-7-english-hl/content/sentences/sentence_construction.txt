INTRO: Sentences are built in three ways. A <strong>simple</strong> sentence has one finite verb, a <strong>compound</strong> sentence joins equal ideas, and a <strong>complex</strong> sentence has a main clause and a subordinate clause. This revision checks you can tell them apart.

@@CARD
TYPE: spot_difference
TITLE: Three sentence forms
SIDE_A: Form
SIDE_B: Example
CONTENT: Simple, one finite verb and one idea | Anne reads a book.<br>Compound, two or more finite verbs joined by a co-ordinating conjunction | Dan plays soccer at school and works on his computer at home.<br>Complex, a main clause and a subordinate clause joined by a subordinating conjunction | Candice received her results while she was recuperating in hospital.
EXAMPLE: Count the finite verbs, then check how the clauses are joined.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Joining words
CONTENT: Co-ordinating conjunctions such as and, but, or and so join equal clauses in a compound sentence. Subordinating conjunctions such as because, while, although, if and after join a subordinate clause to a main clause in a complex sentence.
EXAMPLE: and, but, or, so<br>because, while, although, if, after, when
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: step_by_step
TITLE: Naming the sentence form
CONTENT: 1. Underline each finite verb.<br>2. If there is one, the sentence is simple.<br>3. If there are two or more equal clauses joined by and, but, or or so, it is compound.<br>4. If a subordinating conjunction makes one clause depend on another, it is complex.
EXAMPLE: She stayed in because it was raining.<br>Two verbs and the conjunction because, so it is complex.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Long is not complex
CONTENT: A sentence can be long and still be simple if it has only one finite verb. Length does not decide the form. The verbs and conjunctions do.
EXAMPLE: The tall girl in the red jersey walked slowly across the field. (simple)
@@ENDCARD

Which word is a co-ordinating conjunction?
A: because
B: but
C: while
D: although
CORRECT: B
EXPLAIN:
But joins equal clauses. Because, while and although introduce subordinate clauses.

A: and
B: because
C: but
D: or
CORRECT: B
EXPLAIN:
Because introduces a subordinate clause. And, but and or join equal clauses.

QUESTION:
Which sentence is complex?
A: It was cold so they played outside.
B: It was cold, but they played outside.
C: It was cold and they played outside.
D: Although it was cold, they played outside.
CORRECT: D
EXPLAIN:
Although is a subordinating conjunction, so the first clause depends on the second. In the others, but, and and so join equal clauses.

B: and
C: because

EXPLAIN:
Because makes it rained a subordinate clause that gives the reason. And, but and or would join equal clauses.
