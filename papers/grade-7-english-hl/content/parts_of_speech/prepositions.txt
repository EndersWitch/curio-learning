INTRO: A <strong>preposition</strong> is a small word that shows how one thing relates to another. It usually comes before a noun or a pronoun and tells us about place, position, time or manner.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: What is a preposition?
CONTENT: A preposition shows the relationship of one thing to another. It usually comes before a noun, a pronoun, an article or a gerund.
EXAMPLE: The computer is on the table.<br>He went with Peter.<br>His love of fishing kept him busy.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Simple prepositions
CONTENT: Simple prepositions are single small words. The Handbook says there are no fixed rules for them, so we learn them from usage and reading.
EXAMPLE: in, on, at, under, over, beside, behind, between, among, near, with, by, for, from, to, of
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: spot_difference
TITLE: Preposition or adverb
SIDE_A: Word type
SIDE_B: Example
CONTENT: Preposition, a noun or pronoun follows | The dog was barking outside the house.<br>She rested after the meal.<br>Adverb, nothing follows | We put the dog outside.<br>She did it afterwards.
EXAMPLE: A noun or pronoun must follow a preposition. If it does not, the word is an adverb.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: The pronoun after a preposition
CONTENT: After a preposition, use the object form of the pronoun, such as me, him, her or them. The subject forms I, he, she and they do not belong here.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: She went with he.<br>Right: She went with him.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Find the preposition
CONTENT: Write the preposition in this sentence and say what it shows. The cat slept under the bed.
EXAMPLE: under. It shows where the cat slept in relation to the bed.
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:

@@ENDLEVEL

Which word is a preposition? The computer is on the table.
A: is
B: computer
C: table
D: on
CORRECT: D
EXPLAIN:
On shows where the computer is in relation to the table. Computer and table are nouns, and is is a verb.

CORRECT: C
EXPLAIN:
A preposition shows how one thing relates to another, such as where it is or when it happens. Actions are shown by verbs, qualities by adjectives and frequency by adverbs.
@@ENDQ

INTRO: Some prepositions are made of two small words joined together, and others are whole <strong>groups of words</strong> that work as one preposition. This level helps you spot them and choose the right one.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: Compound prepositions
CONTENT: A compound preposition is made by joining two words into one. It works like any other preposition and is followed by a noun or pronoun.
EXAMPLE: into, onto, within, without, upon, inside, throughout<br>The cat fell into the stream.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: Complex prepositions
CONTENT: A complex preposition is a group of words that works as one preposition. The Handbook notes that phrases may contain more than one preposition.
EXAMPLE: on behalf of, in accordance with, in front of, because of, instead of, in spite of, according to<br>I am writing on behalf of my friend.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Between and among
CONTENT: Use between when something is shared by two people or things. Use among when it is shared by several.
EXAMPLE: The sweets were shared between the two sisters.<br>The books were divided among the children.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Choosing the group of words
CONTENT: Treat the whole group as one preposition. Learn it as a unit, because changing one word gives a different meaning or no meaning at all.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: You must play in accordance of the rules.<br>Right: You must play in accordance with the rules.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Find the group
CONTENT: Write the group of words that works as one preposition. We stayed inside because of the rain.
EXAMPLE: because of. It is followed by the rain and shows the reason.
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:
Recognise compound prepositions
Recognise groups of words that work as one preposition
Choose between and among
Complete sentences with the correct group of words
Count the words in a complex preposition
@@ENDLEVEL

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Prepositions of time
CONTENT: A preposition of time tells when. The Handbook says prepositions are learned from usage, so these are common patterns rather than strict rules.
EXAMPLE: during winter, after the meal, before breakfast, by noon<br>The concert is in the evening.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Prepositions of place
CONTENT: A preposition of place tells where something is in relation to something else.
EXAMPLE: The books are in the suitcase.<br>She stood against the wall.<br>The dustbin is behind the door.<br>She walked along the path.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Manner and reason
CONTENT: A preposition of manner tells how or by what means something is done. A preposition of reason tells why.
EXAMPLE: She travelled by bus.<br>We prevent disease by vaccination.<br>She was inspired by her teacher.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: spot_difference
TITLE: One word, different jobs
SIDE_A: Sentence
SIDE_B: What by shows

EXAMPLE: Read the whole sentence to decide what a preposition shows.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Fixed combinations
CONTENT: Many adjectives and verbs go with one particular preposition. Learn them as a pair.
EXAMPLE: afraid of, proud of, fond of, good at, keen on, interested in, tired of<br>believe in, rely on, wait for, agree with, succeed in
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Complete the pair
CONTENT: Choose the correct preposition. We are proud ___ our school.
EXAMPLE: of. Proud of is the fixed combination.
@@ENDCARD
