INTRO: Punctuation makes writing easier to read. This level covers <strong>capital letters</strong>, the marks that end a sentence and the use of <strong>commas</strong>.

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Capital letters
CONTENT: Every sentence begins with a capital letter. Proper nouns, such as names of people and places, also need capital letters.
EXAMPLE: We visited Cape Town last year.<br>My teacher, Mrs Jones, comes from England.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Ending a sentence
CONTENT: A statement ends with a full stop. A question ends with a question mark, and an exclamation ends with an exclamation mark.
EXAMPLE: We bought popcorn.<br>Who won the match?<br>Ouch!
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Commas in a list
CONTENT: Commas separate the items in a list. There is no comma before and or or.
EXAMPLE: We bought books, pens, pencils and erasers.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Add the commas
CONTENT: Add the commas to this sentence. We packed apples oranges bananas and grapes.
EXAMPLE: We packed apples, oranges, bananas and grapes.
@@ENDCARD

INTRO: Punctuation marks make writing easier to read and understand. This Grade 7 revision covers capital letters, commas, semi-colons, colons, quotation marks, apostrophes, hyphens, dashes and ellipses.

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Capitals, full stops and commas
CONTENT: Sentences begin with capital letters, and so do proper nouns and the main words in titles. Commas separate items in a list, with no comma before and or or. They also separate introductory words and extra information such as however or a person's title.
EXAMPLE: We bought books, pens, pencils and erasers.<br>She was, however, late.<br>Mr Jenkins, the Principal, addressed the parents.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Semi-colons, colons and the comma splice
CONTENT: Do not use a comma to join two main clauses, which is a comma splice. Use a full stop, a semi-colon or a conjunction. A semi-colon balances two related ideas. A colon introduces a list, an explanation or a quotation.
EXAMPLE: A census was taken. The government needed statistics.<br>In summer she swims; in winter she skates.<br>We need to buy the following items: lettuce, tomatoes and carrots.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Apostrophes
CONTENT: An apostrophe shows where letters are left out in a contraction or shows possession. If the owner is singular, add 's. If the owners are plural and end in s, add the apostrophe after the s. Possessive pronouns such as ours and theirs take no apostrophe.
EXAMPLE: didn't, we've<br>the boy's school, the boys' school<br>women's clothing
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Quotation marks, hyphens, dashes and ellipses
CONTENT: Quotation marks enclose direct speech, and all punctuation falls inside them. A hyphen links a prefix or two words, and is used when a prefix ends in the vowel that begins the word. A dash gives a pause or afterthought. An ellipsis shows that a sentence is incomplete.
EXAMPLE: Mr Thompson said, "There will be no homework this weekend."<br>re-examine, co-opt<br>He walked to the edge of the cliff and ...
@@ENDCARD

TESTED:
Use capital letters correctly
Use commas, semi-colons and colons
Avoid the comma splice
Use apostrophes for contractions and possession
Use quotation marks, hyphens, dashes and ellipses
@@ENDLEVEL
