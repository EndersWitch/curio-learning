INTRO: <strong>Direct speech</strong> uses the exact words of the speaker. <strong>Reported speech</strong> tells what was said without quoting it. This Grade 7 revision checks punctuation, pronouns, tenses and time words.

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Direct speech
CONTENT: Direct speech uses the exact words of the speaker, inside quotation marks. The introductory verb, such as said or asked, is followed by a comma. The first spoken word begins with a capital letter, and each new speaker starts a new paragraph.
EXAMPLE: Mary said, "It is cold today."<br>"How long until half-time?" asked an excited spectator.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: step_by_step
TITLE: Changing to reported speech
CONTENT: 1. Remove the quotation marks, question marks, exclamation marks and capital letters.<br>2. Add that, if or whether, or keep the question word.<br>3. Change the pronouns so the meaning stays the same.<br>4. Put the verb one stage back in tense when the introductory verb is in the past.<br>5. Change the time and place words.
EXAMPLE: Joanne said, "I am feeling dizzy today."<br>Joanne said that she was feeling dizzy that day.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: spot_difference
TITLE: Time and place words
SIDE_A: Direct
SIDE_B: Reported
CONTENT: here | there<br>now | then<br>today | that day<br>yesterday | the day before or the previous day<br>tomorrow | the next day or the following day<br>two days ago | two days before
EXAMPLE: These words change when the introductory verb is in the past tense.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Questions, commands and exclamations
CONTENT: Questions that begin with a question word keep that word. Yes or no questions take if or whether. Commands become to plus the verb. Exclamations such as ouch and hooray are described rather than repeated.
EXAMPLE: Roxanne asked why she couldn't have another candy floss.<br>The teacher ordered the children to do their homework.<br>The girl screamed in pain that she had stubbed her toe.
@@ENDCARD

QUESTION:
Direct speech uses
A: only questions
B: a summary of what was said
C: no punctuation
D: the exact words of the speaker
CORRECT: D
EXPLAIN:
Direct speech repeats the speaker's own words. Reported speech gives a summary.

QUESTION:
Which sentence is reported speech?
A: "It is cold," said Mary.
B: Mary said, "It is cold."
C: Mary said that it was cold.
D: Mary asked, "Is it cold?"
CORRECT: C
EXPLAIN:
It does not quote the exact words, and it uses that and the past tense. The others are direct speech.

QUESTION:
Which is the correct reported form? The teacher ordered, "Children, do your homework!"
A: The teacher ordered that children do your homework.
B: The teacher ordered the children do your homework.
C: The teacher ordered, the children to do their homework!
D: The teacher ordered the children to do their homework.
CORRECT: D
EXPLAIN:
A command is reported with to and the verb, and your changes to their. The other versions keep wrong pronouns or punctuation.

QUESTION:
Which is the correct reported form of Shahida said, "My family and I are going to the flea market today."?
A: Shahida said that she and her family would be going to the flea market that day.
B: Shahida said that my family and I are going to the flea market today.
C: Shahida said that she and her family are going to the flea market today.
D: Shahida said that she and her family were going to the flea market today.
CORRECT: A
EXPLAIN:
The pronouns change to she and her, the verb goes back a stage, and today becomes that day. The other versions miss at least one change.

Which sentence has the spoken words first and is punctuated correctly?
A: "How long until half-time?" asked an excited spectator.
B: "How long until half-time." asked an excited spectator.
C: "How long until half-time?", asked an excited spectator.
D: "how long until half-time?" asked an excited spectator.

EXPLAIN:
When the spoken words come first they end with a comma, question mark or exclamation mark, never a full stop. The follow-on words begin with a small letter unless a name follows.
