WHY DO HIPPOS LIVE IN WATER? 
A very long time ago in Africa, when all the animals lived 
together in the bush with the Creator, it was very hot and the 
sun baked the earth every day. 
All the animals had strong skins to protect them against the sun: 
fur, feathers or scales. Hippo's skin was not that strong.  As he 
grew bigger, his skin stretched and became thinner. When he became an adult, his skin 
was so thin that it was easily burned by the sun.  Every day Hippo suffered. His skin 
dried and cracked, causing him a lot of pain and discomfort. 
One day when Hippo could not endure it anymore, he went to the Creator and 
asked, "Please, may I live in the river?"  The Creator looked with kindness at Hippo and 
said, "Yes my friend, you may, but as with all things, you must first ask permission from 
the river animals." 
Hippo asked the river animals, Fish Eagle, Crocodile and Otter, "Please may I come and 
live in the river with you. Look at my thin skin! It is cracked and very painful. The cool 
water will bring great comfort to me." 
The river animals were a bit selfish and also worried. After thinking about it for a short 
time, they said to Hippo, “No, we cannot allow this! Look at you! You are so big and will 
eat all the fish in the river. There will be no more food for us." 
Hippo said to the river animals, "You do not have to worry, as I do not eat fish. I will only 
eat the grass and river plants."  
The river animals were very doubtful and did not believe this, so Hippo told them, "I 
promise to open my mouth wide every day, so that you all can see there are no fish 
bones or scales in my mouth. I will spread my dung with my tail, so that you can see 
there are no bones." 
This convinced the river animals. From that day, the river animals allowed Hippo to live 
in the river, and Hippo would always open his mouth wide and spread his dung with his 
tail. 
                                             [Adapted from: African Folktales and Legends by G Lee]

Mammals 
Mammals are the largest animals on our planet. They can regulate their own body temperature 
and circulatory system.   Mammals are known as warm-blooded animals which allow them to live 
in almost any climate on earth. All female mammals feed their young with milk, which is secreted 
from special glands called the mammary glands. This ability to produce milk to feed their babies 
allows them to spend more time with their young and teach them the important skills they need to 
survive. 
There are more than 4 000 different species of mammals. The smallest mammal is the hog-nosed 
bat whilst the largest is the blue whale which can grow up to 30 metres long and weighs 150 tons. 
Whether they live on land or water, all mammals share some common characteristics: they have 
hair on their bodies and they are vertebrates (which means they have a backbone or spine). 
[Adapted from Wikipedia]
