INTRO: Some words look and sound exactly the same but mean different things. These words are called <strong>homonyms</strong>. The sentence around the word, called its context, tells us which meaning is meant.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: What are homonyms?
CONTENT: Homonyms are words that are spelt and pronounced in an identical way but have different meanings and functions. You may be asked to use a homonym in a sentence as a noun, an adjective or a verb.
EXAMPLE: The dog has a loud bark.<br>The bark of the tree showed its age.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Context decides the meaning
CONTENT: A homonym on its own can mean several things. Read the whole sentence, and notice what job the word does, to know which meaning is intended.
EXAMPLE: The bat flew into the cave. (a noun, the animal)<br>He will bat first for his team. (a verb)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: More homonyms
CONTENT: The same spelling can be a different part of speech, and the meaning changes with it.
EXAMPLE: She loved her birthday present. (noun)<br>James will present the trophy. (verb)<br>Many people were present. (adjective)<br>I need medication for my cold. (noun, an illness)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: spot_difference
TITLE: Homonym or homophone
SIDE_A: Type
SIDE_B: Example
CONTENT: Homonym, same spelling and same sound | bark (of a dog) and bark (of a tree)<br>Homophone, different spelling but same sound | to, too and two
EXAMPLE: A homonym looks identical. A homophone only sounds identical.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Two meanings
CONTENT: Write two different meanings of the word peer. Use these sentences. It is rude to peer through the window. Your peer group influences your thinking.
EXAMPLE: To look closely. A person of the same age or standing.
@@ENDCARD

@@Q
DIFFICULTY: Starter
QUESTION:
What are homonyms?
A: Words with similar meanings
B: Words that sound the same but are spelt differently
C: Words with opposite meanings
D: Words spelt and pronounced the same, with different meanings
CORRECT: D
EXPLAIN:
Homonyms look and sound identical but mean different things. Words that sound alike but are spelt differently are homophones, opposites are antonyms and similar words are synonyms.
@@ENDQ

QUESTION:
In The dog has a loud bark and The bark of the tree showed its age, the word bark is an example of
A: an antonym
B: a homonym
C: a synonym
D: a prefix
CORRECT: B
EXPLAIN:
Bark is spelt and pronounced the same in both sentences but has different meanings, so it is a homonym.
@@ENDQ

A: mat
B: cat
C: hat
D: bat

EXPLAIN:
Bat can mean an animal, a cricket bat, or the verb to bat. Cat, hat and mat each have only one common meaning.

What helps us choose the meaning of a homonym?
A: The first letter
B: The length of the word
C: The context
D: The font

EXPLAIN:
The words around a homonym show which meaning is intended. The length, first letter and font do not change the meaning.

@@CARD
TYPE: spot_difference
TITLE: Homophones and homonyms
SIDE_A: Type
SIDE_B: Example
CONTENT: Homophone, sounds the same but spelt differently | hear and here<br>their, there and they're<br>to, too and two<br>Homonym, spelt and sounds the same | bark (dog) and bark (tree)<br>bat (animal) and bat (verb)
EXAMPLE: Check the spelling to decide which one you have.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Choose by meaning
CONTENT: For homophones, decide what the sentence means and pick the spelling that has that meaning. For homonyms, read the context to see which meaning is meant.
EXAMPLE: Take care not to lose your passport.<br>This dress is loose because I have lost weight.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: step_by_step
TITLE: Working out the right word
CONTENT: 1. Read the whole sentence.<br>2. Decide what job the word does.<br>3. Think of the meaning that fits.<br>4. Choose the spelling for that meaning.
EXAMPLE: The driver had to brake suddenly.<br>Brake means to slow down a vehicle, so it is the right spelling.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Common mix-ups
CONTENT: The most common mistakes are using their, there and they're for one another, mixing up lose and loose, and confusing whole and hole.
EXAMPLE: Wrong: Take care not to loose your passport.<br>Right: Take care not to lose your passport.
@@ENDCARD
