FOR EDUCATORS’ USE: MARKING GUIDELINES FOR QUESTIONS 1 AND 2 
o Spelling errors in one-mark-answers will only be penalised if they change the 
meaning of the answer. 
o Spelling and language errors in longer answers cannot be penalised as the focus is 
on comprehension. 
o For open-ended questions, one cannot award marks for the YES or NO or I AGREE 
or I DISAGREE. The reason or motivation must be considered. 
o For TRUE or FALSE or FACT or OPINION questions, one mark is awarded for the 
reason or motivation or quote. 
- 
If the learner has answered TRUE or FALSE correctly, but the motivation is incorrect, 
they are awarded only ONE mark. 
- 
If the learner has answered the TRUE or FALSE incorrectly, but the motivation is 
correct, NO MARKS are awarded. 
o No penalising the lack of quotation marks, even if a quote was asked for. 
o Where a one-word-answer is requested and the candidate provides the answer in 
a full sentence, it is accepted, provided that the correct answer is underlined or 
highlighted in some way. 
o When two or three facts or marks are required and the candidate provides a 
whole list, only the first two or three facts will be marked. 
o Should the candidate use another language, other than the one used in the 
question paper, the foreign words must be ignored.   
- 
Should the answer, without the foreign words, make sense, the candidate will not 
be penalised. 
- 
The answer will also be accepted if it requires a foreign word in the answer. 
o Accept dialectal variations. 
o For multiple choice questions, BOTH the letter AND the correct answer, written in 
full, must be accepted. 
IT IS ESSENTIAL THAT THESE GUIDELINES BE ADHERED TO ACROSS THE GRADE. 
CONSISTENCY AND FAIRNESS MUST BE PRIORITISED.

FOR EDUCATORS’ USE: MARKING GUIDELINES FOR QUESTION 4 
o One-word-answers are correct despite incorrect spelling unless the error 
changes the meaning of the word.  
o Incorrect spelling in sentences must be penalised if the error occurs within the 
sentence construction that is being tested. 
o The use of correct punctuation with abbreviations is essential. 
o Sentence structures must be grammatically correct and in full sentences, as 
per instructions. 
o Both the letter and the corresponding answer are acceptable with multiple 
choice questions. 
 
WORD STRUCTURES 
o Spelling must be correct for the mark to be awarded. 
o The standard variant applies. 
o If only one word is required as an answer, the learner does not receive any 
marks if the instruction is not followed. However, if the learner writes a 
sentence, but identifies the correct word by underlining or highlighting it, 
award the mark. 
 
SENTENCE STRUCTURES 
o Spelling does not count. 
o Regional language or dialect cannot be taken into consideration. 
o Should the instruction indicate that the learner must begin the sentence in a 
specific manner, the WHOLE sentence must be written out in full. 
o PLEASE NOTE: With regard to advertisements and cartoons, language and 
spelling errors do not count, unless they impact on the meaning of the 
sentence. 
AGAIN, CONSISTENCY IN TERMS OF THE MARKING GUIDELINES WILL 
ENSURE FAIRNESS AND WILL ASSIST WITH THE POST-MODERATION OF 
MARKED SCRIPTS.
