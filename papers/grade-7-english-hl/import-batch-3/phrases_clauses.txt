INTRO: Groups of words build sentences. A <strong>phrase</strong> has no finite verb, and a <strong>clause</strong> has one. A clause can be a main clause or a subordinate clause.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: Phrase
CONTENT: A phrase is a group of words without a finite verb. It conveys a single idea and can never stand alone.
EXAMPLE: The shop around the corner
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: Clause
CONTENT: A clause is a group of words that contains a finite verb. Underline the finite verbs to count the clauses.
EXAMPLE: The van came to a halt.<br>after it had run out of petrol
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Main and subordinate
CONTENT: A main clause can stand alone and makes complete sense. A subordinate clause has a verb but cannot stand alone.
EXAMPLE: The van came to a halt (main) after it had run out of petrol (subordinate).
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Phrase or clause
CONTENT: Say whether this group of words is a phrase or a clause. in the old house
EXAMPLE: A phrase, because it has no finite verb.
@@ENDCARD

INTRO: A <strong>phrase</strong> has no finite verb, and a <strong>clause</strong> has one. This Grade 7 revision checks that you can tell them apart, find main and subordinate clauses, and name the type of each.

@@CARD
TYPE: spot_difference
TITLE: Phrase and clause
SIDE_A: Group of words
SIDE_B: Example
CONTENT: Phrase, no finite verb | The shop around the corner<br>Clause, has a finite verb | the van came to a halt<br>Main clause, can stand alone | The van came to a halt<br>Subordinate clause, cannot stand alone | after it had run out of petrol
EXAMPLE: Underline the finite verbs. They tell you how many clauses there are.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Types of phrase
CONTENT: Adjectival phrases tell us more about a noun. Noun phrases answer who or what. Adverbial phrases modify the verb and tell how, when, where or why.
EXAMPLE: The model with the dazzling smile came from Durban. (adjectival)<br>The girl entered the room at nine o'clock. (adverbial, time)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Types of subordinate clause
CONTENT: A noun clause does the job of a noun and can be replaced by it or that. An adjectival clause qualifies a noun and usually begins with who, which, that, whom or whose. An adverbial clause does the job of an adverb and tells time, place, manner, reason, concession, result or condition.
EXAMPLE: What they learnt at school helped them. (noun)<br>The girl who had the longest hair won. (adjectival)<br>While the engine was running, the man jumped out. (adverbial, time)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: A group of words is not always a clause
CONTENT: A group of words with no finite verb is a phrase, however long it is. A clause must have a finite verb of its own.
EXAMPLE: The shop around the corner is a phrase.<br>The shop is open is a clause.
@@ENDCARD
