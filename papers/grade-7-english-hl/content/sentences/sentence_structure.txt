INTRO: Every sentence is built from parts. The <strong>subject</strong> is who or what the sentence is about, the <strong>verb</strong> is the action, and the <strong>object</strong> receives the action.

@@CARD
TYPE: definition
TITLE: The subject
CONTENT: The subject is who or what we are talking about. To find it, ask who or what before the verb.
EXAMPLE: The boy plays soccer.<br>Who plays? The boy.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: The verb and the object
CONTENT: The verb is the action or state. The direct object answers who or what after the verb.
EXAMPLE: Anne reads a book.<br>Verb, reads. Direct object, a book.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: The predicate
CONTENT: The predicate is the rest of the sentence after the subject. It contains the verb and says something about the subject.
EXAMPLE: The dog | chased the cat.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: try_it
TITLE: Find the subject
CONTENT: Write the subject of this sentence. The girls sang loudly.
EXAMPLE: The girls. Ask who sang.
@@ENDCARD

INTRO: You have worked on <strong>subject</strong> and <strong>object</strong> before. This Grade 7 revision adds the <strong>predicate</strong>, the <strong>complement</strong> and the two kinds of object in longer sentences.

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Subject and predicate
CONTENT: A sentence can be divided into a subject and a predicate. The subject is who or what we are talking about and usually performs the action. The predicate is the rest of the sentence and contains the verb.
EXAMPLE: The boys in Grade 7 | were happy to go to high school.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Objects and complement
CONTENT: The direct object answers who or what after the verb. The indirect object tells to whom or for whom the action is done. The word that follows a linking verb such as be is not an object but a complement.
EXAMPLE: She threw her brother the ball. (direct object, the ball; indirect object, her brother)<br>The race was exciting. (complement, exciting)
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: step_by_step
TITLE: Analysing a sentence
CONTENT: 1. Find the finite verb.<br>2. Ask who or what before the verb to find the subject.<br>3. Ask who or what after the verb to find the direct object.<br>4. Ask to whom or for whom to find the indirect object.<br>5. Everything after the subject is the predicate.
EXAMPLE: Anne reads a book.<br>Subject, Anne. Verb, reads. Direct object, a book.
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: common_mistake
TITLE: Linking verbs have no object
CONTENT: After a linking verb such as be or become, the word that follows names or describes the subject. It is a complement, not an object.
EXAMPLE: The author became famous.<br>Famous is the complement.
@@ENDCARD
