INTRO: Good spellers know the <strong>rules</strong> for adding endings to words, and good readers know how to use a <strong>dictionary</strong>. This Grade 7 revision checks both.

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Adding suffixes
CONTENT: After a short vowel and a consonant, double the consonant before a vowel suffix. After a long vowel, simply join. If a word ends in e, drop the e before a vowel suffix, but keep it after ce or ge. If a word ends in y, change the y to i before a suffix.
EXAMPLE: sit + ing = sitting, hop + ed = hopped<br>fame + ous = famous<br>service + able = serviceable<br>rely + able = reliable, heavy + ness = heaviness
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Two-syllable words and ful
CONTENT: In a word of two syllables, double the final consonant if the stress is on the second syllable, but not if the stress is on the first. When you add the suffix -ful, remember it has only one l.
EXAMPLE: regret + ing = regretting<br>market + ing = marketing<br>beauty + ful = beautiful
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: i before e
CONTENT: The usual rule is i before e except after c. There are exceptions, and the Handbook lists foreign, height, heir, leisure, seize, weight and weird.
EXAMPLE: believe, receive<br>weird, seize, height
@@ENDCARD

@@CARD
TYPE: key_rule
TITLE: Dictionary skills
CONTENT: Words in a dictionary are arranged alphabetically. The guide words, or headwords, at the top of a page show the first and last word on that page. A dictionary gives spelling, meaning, pronunciation and word usage, and a thesaurus gives synonyms.
EXAMPLE: A page with the guide words apple and bake contains avocado but not cabbage.
@@ENDCARD
